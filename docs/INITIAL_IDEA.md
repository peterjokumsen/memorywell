This is a beautiful concept-essentially a "digital time capsule" or a "collaborative legacy journal." By shifting the focus from a solo parenting task to a multi-generational shared experience, you turn a storage tool into a living family heirloom.
Here is a breakdown of how to approach this from a product and feature perspective:

1. The Core Concept: "The Circle of Care"
   Instead of just a "User and Profile" model, think of it as a "Chronicle" (the child's record) and a "Contributors" (the relatives).

   - The Owner (Parent): Curates the timeline, manages permissions, and sets the "tone" of the record.
   - The Contributors (Grandparents, Aunts, Uncles): Receive specific invites to add "Moments."
   - The Recipient (The Child): The eventual "Read-Only" owner who unlocks the full archive on a specific milestone (e.g., 18th birthday).

2. Feature Set & User Experience
   A. The Invitation System (The "Extender")
   To make it easy for non-tech-savvy relatives, the invite process must be frictionless.

   - Magic Links: Parents generate a unique link (sent via WhatsApp/iMessage). The relative clicks it and is immediately taken to a "Drop Zone" without needing to create a full, complex account (or using a very lightweight social login).
   - Role-Based Permissions:
   - Full Contributor: Can upload text, photos, and voice notes.
   - Observer: Can only view (perhaps for older relatives who just want to see updates).
   - The "Prompt" System: To avoid the "I don't know what to write" problem, send relatives monthly or quarterly prompts: "What is one funny thing Child's Name did this week?" or "What is a wish you have for them?"
     B. Content Types (Optimized for Storage)
     Since you want to avoid the storage bloat of video, you can implement:
   - Text: Long-form stories, "Letters to my future self," or quick micro-journal entries.
   - Photos: High-quality compressed images. You might include a "Photo of the Day" feature.
   - Voice Notes: This is your "secret sauce." Hearing a grandparent's voice from 15 years ago is often more emotionally resonant than a video. It's low-bandwidth but high-emotion.
     C. The Timeline View
     The child shouldn't just see a list of files; they should see a Storyline.
   - Chronological Feed: A scrollable journey from birth to present.
   - "Contributor Threads": A way to see all the things Grandma wrote vs. all the things Dad wrote.
   - Milestone Chapters: Automatically group entries into "The Baby Years," "The Toddler Years," etc.

3. Technical & Strategic Considerations
   Challenge Solution
   Storage Costs Use a tiered storage approach. Use highly compressed formats for photos and mono-channel AAC/MP3 for voice notes to keep footprints tiny.
   Privacy & Safety This is the #1 concern. Implement end-to-end encryption or at least "Private by Design" architecture. The data should never be indexable by search engines.
   Data Longevity The biggest question: "Will this work in 20 years?" Use open standards (JPEG, MP3, JSON/Text) rather than proprietary formats so the data remains readable as technology evolves.
   The "Empty Wall" Problem If a relative is invited but never posts, the record looks lonely. Use "Nudges"-gentle notifications to contributors when a significant milestone (like a first word) is logged.
4. Potential Monetization / Growth Models
   - Freemium: Free for text and a limited number of photos; a "Legacy Subscription" for unlimited voice notes and high-res photos.
   - The Physical Pivot: At the end of 18 years, offer a service to print the "Year 1-5" collection into a high-quality physical coffee table book.
